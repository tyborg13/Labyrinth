## References are for camera, scale and hand only
Never copy a reference's pose, silhouette, species or equipment. The new subject must have its own distinct silhouette
that could not be mistaken for any reference at game size.

## Scale and detail (most important)
The reference images are sprites from this game enlarged 4x with hard pixel edges: every small square block you see in
them is ONE game pixel (4x4 image pixels). Paint the new subject in exactly the same block size and the same hand:
- every colour patch is at least one 4x4 block; contours step in 4-pixel blocks; outline is one dark block wide.
- the same LOW level of detail as the references: a few big, chunky, readable masses per body part. Nothing thinner
  than one block. No fine texture, no thin strands or filaments, no tiny repeated details (scales, stitches, chain
  links, rivets, cracks) unless each piece is at least 3 blocks wide.
- The image will be shrunk 4x back to game size; whatever needs a block smaller than 4 pixels will vanish, so leave it out.

## Proportions (most important change)
Follow the maquette's proportions exactly. A long, fat, soft-bodied burrowing grub LYING ON THE GROUND under its own
weight: a big round head whose whole front is one circular maw ringed with teeth, then six fat ridged body rings that
swell slightly toward the middle, ending in a blunt tail ring. The body does NOT arch. Its belly rests on the ground
along its whole length, so the lower edge of the body is one continuous soft line from the head to the tail, with no
gap and no daylight under it, running at the angle the maquette shows. Six very short, thick, hooked stubs are spread
evenly along the body, one pair each on the first, third and fifth rings, not bunched at the front. They barely clear
the body's lower edge: the body is NOT held up on them. Every ring is a thick separate band at least 4 blocks wide.

## Palette and light
Muted, dark, desaturated dark-fantasy palette like the references. Values are mostly dark and mid-dark: no large area is
lighter than the hero's face; skin, bone and cloth are dusky, never pale, tan or bright. Light from the upper left.
Exactly ONE clear accent colour (the bone-yellow teeth), used sparingly.
Paint like the references, not flat cartoon shading: every big mass has three or four value steps with warm brown-tinted
shadows, a little worn texture and grime inside the big shapes. Avoid flat neutral grey as a main colour (grey skin is
grey-brown and mottled); cool colours belong only to ice, steel or the accent.
Gaunt or wretched creatures stay gaunt in character, but are still drawn with bold shapes: big head, big hands and
feet, limbs at least 3 blocks thick. Chunky does not mean muscular.

## Background
Perfectly flat opaque chroma green #00FF00 (RGB 0,255,0) on every background pixel, including gaps between limbs.
No floor, ground, cast shadow, glow halo, vignette, scenery, border, text or labels. Nothing touches the canvas edge.

## Structure (most important): the maquette sets pose and perspective only
The first reference is a simple 3D maquette of this exact pose, rendered from the game camera. Its flat colours are PART
LABELS, not paint colours. Keep every part where the maquette puts it: the silhouette, size, overlaps, camera angle and
which side each limb is on. Never move, merge or invent parts, and keep each part visibly separate where the maquette
separates it (the rig will cut along these parts).
Labels: salmon red = the head with its round toothed maw (the tooth ring is ONE feature that stays whole on the head);
the six differently coloured fat rings behind it (blue, yellow-green, magenta, mint, tan, violet) = body segments 1 to 6
from head to rear; green = the blunt tail ring; the three coloured stalks in front of the body are the near legs (pink,
light blue, yellow, front to back). The small purple scrap beyond the head is the tip of a far leg.
The second reference shows the same maquette's form in grey. Its lumpy surface is a sculpting artefact: do NOT paint
lumps, craters or noise. Do not reproduce the label colours.

## Forms: redraw from the third reference, not from the maquette
The third reference is this creature's current painting. Redraw every form the way it is drawn there, in the maquette's
new pose. Each ring has the same smooth, tight, overlapping hide with a darker groove at the seam and the same blotched
markings. The maw is the same: a raised fleshy lip, a slick dark throat and a ring of thick yellowed teeth. Each leg is
a short version of the same chunky, ringed leg ending in one hooked claw, no longer than the maquette's stub and growing
from the same place low on the flank. No spheres and no peg shapes. No
hard corners and no long straight parallel edges in the silhouette.

## Identity and materials
Keep the third reference's identity and materials, NOT its arched pose, its leg placement or its size. Match its dark
values exactly: the hide is a dusky mid-dark brown, not orange or tan, and its lightest areas are no lighter than the
third reference's. Soft, ridged hide in warm earthy browns, mottled with darker blotches; a slick darker maw with a ring of pale yellowed teeth; stubby
dark-brown legs with hooked tips. The ONE accent is the pale bone-yellow of the teeth (#d9c58a). Everything else stays
dark and muted. The fourth reference (the approved Shale Bloomer) shows how this game now paints a creature; the fifth
(the hero) shows the hand, block size and level of detail to match (both enlarged 4x).
