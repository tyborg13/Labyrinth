Use your built-in image generation tool (real image generation, not code drawing). Make ONE image: a square 1024x1024 PNG.

## References are for camera, scale and hand only
Never copy a reference's pose, silhouette, species or equipment. The new subject must have its own distinct silhouette
that could not be mistaken for any reference at game size.

## Scale and detail (most important)
The reference images are sprites from this game enlarged 4x with hard pixel edges: every small square block you see in
them is ONE game pixel (4x4 image pixels). Paint the new subject in exactly the same block size and the same hand:
- every colour patch is at least one 4x4 block; contours step in 4-pixel blocks; outline is one dark block wide.
- the same LOW level of detail as the references: a few big, chunky, readable masses per body part. Nothing thinner
  than one block. No fine texture, no thin strands or filaments, no tiny repeated details (scales, stitches, chain
  links, rivets, cracks) unless each piece is at least 3 blocks wide.
- The image will be shrunk 4x back to game size; whatever needs a block smaller than 4 pixels will vanish, so leave it out.

## Proportions
Stylized and chunky like the hero and the other references, never realistic: humanoids have a big head (about one fifth
of their height), thick limbs at least 3 blocks wide even at wrists and ankles, big hands and feet, a compact torso.
Beasts and objects likewise use heavy, simple masses. Thin, realistic anatomy reads as a miniature at game size.

## Palette and light
Muted, dark, desaturated dark-fantasy palette like the references. Values are mostly dark and mid-dark: no large area is
lighter than the hero's face; skin, bone and cloth are dusky, never pale, tan or bright. Light from the upper left.
Exactly ONE clear accent colour, used sparingly.
Paint like the references, not flat cartoon shading: every big mass has three or four value steps with warm brown-tinted
shadows, a little worn texture and grime inside the big shapes. Avoid flat neutral grey as a main colour (grey skin is
grey-brown and mottled); cool colours belong only to ice, steel or the accent.
Gaunt or wretched creatures stay gaunt in character, but are still drawn with bold shapes: big head, big hands and
feet, limbs at least 3 blocks thick. Chunky does not mean muscular.

## Background
Perfectly flat opaque chroma green #00FF00 (RGB 0,255,0) on every background pixel, including gaps between limbs.
No floor, ground, cast shadow, glow halo, vignette, scenery, border, text or labels. Nothing touches the canvas edge.

## Structure (most important): paint over the maquette
The first reference is a simple 3D maquette of this exact pose, rendered from the game camera. Its flat colours are PART
LABELS, not paint colours. Keep every part where the maquette puts it: the silhouette, size, overlaps, camera angle, the
fighter's crouch with both knees bent and the wide stance, and which side each limb is on. Turn the smooth maquette
shapes into the painted character, but never move, merge or invent parts, and keep each limb visibly separate where the
maquette separates it (the rig will cut along these parts). Labels: yellow = the angular great helm with its crest
(head); coral red = chest and torso, including the scarf and cowl at the neck; dark red = hips; brown = the belt with its
buckle; pink = the front loincloth; dark gold = the far (creature's RIGHT) pauldron; light orange = the near (creature's
LEFT) pauldron, set at the side of the shoulder; violet, lilac and pale pink = the far arm holding the sword: upper arm,
forearm, gauntlet; blue tones = the near arm: upper arm, forearm, fist (lightest); teal tones = legs and sabatons
(lighter = near leg); light grey = the falchion blade. The second reference shows the same maquette's 3D form in grey.
Do not reproduce the label colours.

## Forms
Follow the maquette's masses, which are deliberately heavier than before:
- The helm is ANGULAR, like the third reference: faceted flat planes meeting at hard edges, narrowing toward the top
  into a short raised crest ridge running front to back, with the narrow cross-shaped visor. Not a round cylinder.
- The sword hand is a real grip: armoured fingers wrapped around a short leather-wrapped hilt, with a small crossguard
  between the fist and the blade and a pommel showing below the fist. The blade starts at the crossguard; the hand and
  the sword never melt together.
- The pauldrons sit ON the sides of the shoulders, wrapping over them in overlapping lames, the near one larger and
  turned outward to the side, not pushed forward over the chest.
- The legs are articulated plate armour: a thigh plate (cuisse), a round knee cop with a small side wing, a shin greave
  and a pointed heavy sabaton, with plate edges, straps and buckles, never smooth capsules or tubes.
- Thick armoured arms with round elbow cops: as heavy as the maquette shows them.
- The falchion keeps the maquette's curved blade: a convex cutting edge on the underside and a clipped back near the tip.

## Identity and materials
The third reference is this character's APPROVED painting in almost this exact pose: match its armour, shading,
colours, angular crested helm, sword grip, pauldrons, leg plates, scarf, loincloth and falchion exactly. Where it and the
maquette differ, follow the maquette: here the near arm is a little shorter (the fist sits at the belt) and the feet are
a little closer together. Warm grey-brown gunmetal plate with bronze-brown rims, shaded in three or four value steps with
warm brown shadows and a little grime (dark worn steel, never copper and never blue-grey); a brown leather scarf and
cowl at the neck with a leather cross strap over the chest and a round bronze buckle; brown leather straps at the elbows
and knees; a broad brown leather belt with a square iron buckle; a ragged deep-red cloth loincloth hanging from the belt
(deep and dull, not bright); a big curved falchion of dark steel whose cutting edge glows ember-orange. The glowing ember
edge is the one hot accent. The fourth and fifth references (an approved enemy and the hero, enlarged 4x) show the hand,
block size and level of detail to match.
