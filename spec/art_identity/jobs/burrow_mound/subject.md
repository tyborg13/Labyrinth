Burrow mound: the low heap of freshly churned earth a burrowing grub leaves on the floor tile it dived into. A broad,
rounded mound of dark, damp umber soil, much wider than it is tall (about three times as wide as its height), sitting
corner-on on its diamond footprint and filling about two thirds of it. Big chunky clods of earth, three to five of them
broken off and tumbled down its sides, plus two or three small grey-brown stones pushed up with them. Along the crest
runs one ragged dark crack of loose soil, and through the middle of that crack a short sliver of the grub's own pale
ochre, ringed hide shows: two or three thick segment ridges, the only light-coloured thing on it (ochre #c9a46a is the
one accent). Soil colours stay warm and dark like the dungeon floor: deep umber #2e2116 in the crack and the shadowed
lower-right face, mid brown #4a3520 on the body, dry tan #7a5a34 on the clod tops lit from the upper left. Matte and
grainy, no shine, no grass, no roots, no glow, no outline, no hole you can see into.
