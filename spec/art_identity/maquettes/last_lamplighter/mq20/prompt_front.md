Use your built-in image generation tool (real image generation, not code drawing). Make ONE image: a square 1024x1024 PNG.

## References are for camera, scale and hand only
Never copy a reference's pose, silhouette, species or equipment. The new subject must have its own distinct silhouette
that could not be mistaken for any reference at game size.

## Scale and detail (most important)
The reference images are sprites from this game enlarged 4x with hard pixel edges: every small square block you see in
them is ONE game pixel (4x4 image pixels). Paint the new subject in exactly the same block size and the same hand:
- every colour patch is at least one 4x4 block; contours step in 4-pixel blocks; outline is one dark block wide.
- the same LOW level of detail as the references: a few big, chunky, readable masses per body part. Nothing thinner
  than one block. No fine texture, no thin strands or filaments, no tiny repeated details (scales, stitches, chain
  links, rivets, cracks) unless each piece is at least 3 blocks wide.
- The image will be shrunk 4x back to game size; whatever needs a block smaller than 4 pixels will vanish, so leave it out.

## Proportions
Stylized and chunky like the hero and the other references, never realistic: humanoids have a big head (about one fifth
of their height), thick limbs at least 3 blocks wide even at wrists and ankles, big hands and feet, a compact torso.
Beasts and objects likewise use heavy, simple masses. Thin, realistic anatomy reads as a miniature at game size.

## Palette and light
Muted, dark, desaturated dark-fantasy palette like the references. Values are mostly dark and mid-dark: no large area is
lighter than the hero's face; skin, bone and cloth are dusky, never pale, tan or bright. Light from the upper left.
Exactly ONE clear accent colour, used sparingly.
Paint like the references, not flat cartoon shading: every big mass has three or four value steps with warm brown-tinted
shadows, a little worn texture and grime inside the big shapes. Avoid flat neutral grey as a main colour (grey skin is
grey-brown and mottled); cool colours belong only to ice, steel or the accent.
Gaunt or wretched creatures stay gaunt in character, but are still drawn with bold shapes: big head, big hands and
feet, limbs at least 3 blocks thick. Chunky does not mean muscular.

## Background
Perfectly flat opaque chroma green #00FF00 (RGB 0,255,0) on every background pixel, including gaps between limbs.
No floor, ground, cast shadow, glow halo, vignette, scenery, border, text or labels. Nothing touches the canvas edge.

## Structure (most important): paint over the maquette
The first reference is a simple 3D maquette of this exact pose, rendered from the game camera. Its flat colours are PART
LABELS, not paint colours. Keep every part where the maquette puts it: the silhouette, size, overlaps, camera angle, the
forward stoop and which side each limb is on. Turn the smooth maquette shapes into the painted character, but never move,
merge or invent parts, and keep each limb visibly separate where the maquette separates it (the rig will cut along these
parts). Labels: yellow = the oval mask (face); purple around the mask = the hood; lilac band over both shoulders = the
mantle; the two big rounded squares beside the head = two oversized square iron SHOULDER PLATES, one on each shoulder
(sky blue on the viewer's LEFT = his far, right plate, partly behind the hood; pale yellow on the viewer's RIGHT = his
near, left plate); coral red = chest and torso; dark red = hips; pink = the long robe hanging at the front; gold = the
small birdcage on his chest, the belt and the hanging keys and chains; violet tones on the viewer's LEFT = his far
(RIGHT) arm hanging forward with long claws: upper arm, forearm, clawed hand (lightest); blue tones on the viewer's
RIGHT = his near (LEFT) arm holding the lantern out from his body: upper arm, forearm, fist; pale lavender = the big
hanging lantern below that fist; teal tones = legs and sabatons (lighter = near leg). The second reference shows the
same maquette's 3D form in grey. Do not reproduce the label colours.

## Forms: rounded, not boxy
Every mass is rounded and organic, following the maquette: a deep rounded cowl framing a smooth convex mask, the head
hunched low and forward between the two plates, a soft draped mantle, armoured limbs as heavy segmented iron plates in
overlapping lames with round elbow and knee cops, long clawed iron fingers, a long robe shredded at the hem into long
ragged strands with a few holes, pointed sabatons. Paint every limb and the torso at least as thick as the maquette shows
them, never thinner. The two shoulder plates are thick slabs of dark iron, gently domed, with a raised riveted rim and a
raised cross on their faces, mounted on the shoulders by short iron brackets; their corners are rounded.

## Identity and materials
The third reference is this character's original painting from an earlier, head-on pose: copy its materials, colours and
its two great cross-marked shoulder plates exactly, not its pose. Dark blackened-iron plate with dull bronze edges; a muted
dusky-violet hood and mantle; a pale ivory mask (dusky, not white) with a black cross-shaped eye slit; two oversized
square iron shoulder plates with dark recessed panels and a raised bronze-edged cross; a small dull-brass birdcage on
chains on his chest; a leather belt with hanging chains and two brass keys; a long tattered robe of dark violet-grey
cloth, shredded at the hem; long clawed iron fingers; pointed iron sabatons; a LARGE hexagonal iron lantern (as big as
in the original) hanging on a short chain from a ring gripped in his left fist, with a lit candle inside. The candle's
warm amber glow is the one accent; everything else stays muted and dark. The fourth and fifth references (an approved
enemy and the hero, enlarged 4x) show the hand, block size and level of detail to match.
