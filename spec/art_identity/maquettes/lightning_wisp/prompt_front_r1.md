Use your built-in image generation tool (real image generation, not code drawing). Make ONE image: a square 1024x1024 PNG.

## References are for camera, scale and hand only
Never copy a reference's pose, silhouette, species or equipment. The new subject must have its own distinct silhouette
that could not be mistaken for any reference at game size.

## Scale and detail (most important)
The reference images are sprites from this game enlarged 4x with hard pixel edges: every small square block you see in
them is ONE game pixel (4x4 image pixels). Paint the new subject in exactly the same block size and the same hand:
- every colour patch is at least one 4x4 block; contours step in 4-pixel blocks; outline is one dark block wide.
- the same LOW level of detail as the references: a few big, chunky, readable masses per body part. Nothing thinner
  than one block. No fine texture, no thin strands or filaments, no tiny repeated details (scales, stitches, chain
  links, rivets, cracks) unless each piece is at least 3 blocks wide.
- The image will be shrunk 4x back to game size; whatever needs a block smaller than 4 pixels will vanish, so leave it out.

## Proportions
Stylized and chunky like the hero and the other references, never realistic: humanoids have a big head (about one fifth
of their height), thick limbs at least 3 blocks wide even at wrists and ankles, big hands and feet, a compact torso.
Beasts and objects likewise use heavy, simple masses. Thin, realistic anatomy reads as a miniature at game size.

## Palette and light
Muted, dark, desaturated dark-fantasy palette like the references. Values are mostly dark and mid-dark: no large area is
lighter than the hero's face; skin, bone and cloth are dusky, never pale, tan or bright. Light from the upper left.
Exactly ONE clear accent colour, used sparingly.
Paint like the references, not flat cartoon shading: every big mass has three or four value steps with warm brown-tinted
shadows, a little worn texture and grime inside the big shapes. Avoid flat neutral grey as a main colour (grey skin is
grey-brown and mottled); cool colours belong only to ice, steel or the accent.
Gaunt or wretched creatures stay gaunt in character, but are still drawn with bold shapes: big head, big hands and
feet, limbs at least 3 blocks thick. Chunky does not mean muscular.

## Background
Perfectly flat opaque chroma green #00FF00 (RGB 0,255,0) on every background pixel, including gaps between limbs.
No floor, ground, cast shadow, glow halo, vignette, scenery, border, text or labels. Nothing touches the canvas edge.
## Structure (most important): paint over the maquette
The first reference is a simple 3D maquette of this exact pose, rendered from the game camera. Its flat colours are PART
LABELS, not paint colours. Keep every part where the maquette puts it: the silhouette, size, overlaps, camera angle and
which side each part is on. Turn the smooth maquette shapes into the painted creature, but never move, merge or invent
parts, and keep each part visibly separate where the maquette separates it (the rig will cut along these parts).
Labels: red = the body, a lumpy knot of dark storm cloud; yellow = a crown of short crackling discharge spikes on top;
blue = a forked lightning arc on the image's left; orange = a forked lightning arc on the image's right; teal = a wispy
smoke tail that tapers and curls down to a point (the creature floats; nothing touches the floor). The second reference
shows the same maquette's 3D form in grey. Do not reproduce the label colours.

## Forms: rounded, not boxy
The body is soft, billowing and rounded like a thundercloud: several bulging lobes, never a ball with spikes. The
lightning arcs and crown are jagged but each stroke is at least two blocks thick. The tail curls like smoke.

## Identity and materials
The third reference is this creature's current painting: keep its identity, NOT its pose, size or detail level. A dark
slate-violet storm cloud with deep shadowed folds and dim inner flickers; ONE round glowing eye in the front of the cloud,
pale electric yellow-white; crackling lightning in the same pale electric yellow-white with a thin cool violet fringe.
The lightning and the eye are the ONLY bright accent; the cloud body stays dark and muted (never pale, never fluffy white).
The fourth reference (an approved lightning enemy) shows how this game paints lightning; the fifth (the hero) shows the
hand, block size and level of detail to match (both enlarged 4x).

## Subject: Lightning Wisp (front view)
A floating knot of living storm cloud with a single glowing eye, lightning arcing from its sides, a crown of discharge
crackling on top and a smoky tail trailing below; its eye looks toward the image's lower-left.

## Size
Keep the maquette's size and framing exactly (about 760 pixels tall on the 1024 canvas, arcs included).
