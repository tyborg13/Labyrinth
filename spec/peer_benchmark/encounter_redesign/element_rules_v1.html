Escape the Umbra · encounter design · proposal, 23 October 2026

One rule per element

Each element gets one board rule, and it is the rule that element's cards already use. You learn it once, from your own deck. Every special fight then changes who wields it, where it points, how long it lasts and how big it gets. No special fight adds a keyword of its own.

Wind is the model. The Stormcaller aims it, the Snare blows it at you from the entry, and Vaeloryx turns it into a storm that circles the board. One rule each time: everything not Anchored slides, and a blocked slide is a Collision.

The six rules

All six exist in the game today, with the wording below. Each works the same for you and for them.

Earth

Outcrops

Stone rises on marked tiles and blocks movement. A burst hits everything beside it. Broken stone leaves Rubble.

Your side
Your Earth cards leave Rubble and hit harder on it.
Answer anywhere
Break the stone (it has Health), or stand clear of it when it bursts.
Fire

Fire ground

Entering Fire deals 2. Starting a turn in it deals 3. Detonate makes it erupt and hit everyone in it.

Your side
Your Fire cards place Fire and Detonate it.
Answer anywhere
Ice overwrites Fire. Lure them into their own field.
Air

Wind

At the end of the wielder's turn, everything not Anchored slides with the wind. A blocked slide is a Collision: 2 damage per tile lost.

Your side
Your Air cards Push and Pull under the same Collision rule.
Answer anywhere
Anchor, keep a clear lane downwind, or let it throw them into walls.
Ice

Ice ground

A unit on Ice is Chilled: it takes 2 more damage, and an Ice attack Freezes it.

Your side
Your Ice cards place Ice and Freeze the Chilled.
Answer anywhere
Stay off it, melt it with Fire, or bring them onto it.
Lightning

Electrified ground

A Lightning hit that touches it spreads to everyone on the connected Electrified tiles.

Your side
Your Lightning cards place it and spread through it.
Answer anywhere
Stand off the network when a strike is marked. Your Lightning rides theirs.
Shadow

The Umbra

Beyond your sight, enemies are hidden: no intents, and they can't be targeted. Light reveals them.

Your side
Your Light, Vision and Truesight push it back.
Answer anywhere
Bring Light, fight close, or keep the braziers lit.

What each fight changes

Each fight changes the same few things about the rule: who wields it, where it points, how long it lasts and how big it gets. What the rule does never changes, and neither does the way to stop it.

Warband

The captain wields the rule and aims it. It lasts as long as the captain stands.

Stop it: kill the captain.

Umbral Snare

The room wields the rule against your escape. It always comes from the entry, and it grows every round.

Stop it: get out.

Guardian

One twist on the rule, always on and shown on its boss bar. Two helpers serve the twist.

Stop it: its lever.

Dragon

The rule at full scale. Act two remakes the arena with it. The cataclysm is the rule over the whole board, and its shelter comes from the same rule.

Stop it: Break the cataclysm.

The Ember Hoarder and Last Light are on hold until you've played the Warband and the Snare. When they're built, they take their row from this table the same way: the Hollow Gale gang's wind blows toward the Hoarder's door, and so on.

What gets simpler

  • Six rules to learn, not two dozen. Retired keywords: Bramble (becomes Outcrops), Ice Pillar (becomes Ice ground) and Tether (becomes Electrified ground). The Guardians' custom signatures (Gallows, Crag Armour, Vigil) are dropped, and so is Iskaldra's "Mantle on Ice".
  • Six callouts, total. The first time you meet an element's rule anywhere, cards included, one short card explains it. Bosses don't need their own callouts. Their pre-battle names their element and their one twist.
  • One counter per element, everywhere. Fire melts Ice. Ice smothers Fire. Anchor beats Wind. Light beats the Umbra. Breaking stone beats Outcrops. Staying off the network beats Lightning. A deck built around one element is also built to answer it.

What stays from the boss proposals

  • Dragons: Wrath at half health, a telegraphed cataclysm you can Break, and lieutenants. Now the cataclysm is always the dragon's rule at full scale, and the lieutenants feed that rule.
  • Guardians: one twist each, two finite helpers, and Fury at half health. Storm Cantor and Gallows Roc go back to their designed pace.
  • Clarity: the next-three intent strip for bosses, and the compact Guardian boss bar.
  • Built work to change: four Warband orders (Thornmother, Slagmaster, Rime Oracle, Dynamo) and two Snare pressures (earth, lightning). The Stormcaller, the Prior and the other four pressures already fit.

Your calls

  1. Are these the right six rules, especially Outcrops for earth, Ice ground for ice and Electrified ground for lightning?
  2. Should ordinary enemies follow too? I recommend yes, so the whole game speaks one rule per element: the Briar Weaver grows Outcrops, the Seer's Icefall leaves Ice, and Arc Thralls Electrify the ground.
  3. Pilot: Vaeloryx first, since Wind is built and proven. Then retrofit the four Warband orders and two Snare pressures, then Tharokh and the Guardians.
