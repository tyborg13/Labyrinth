Escape the Umbra · design proposal · revised 20 October 2026

Encounters that ask hard questions

A redesign of the six dragons, the six Guardians, and four special encounters that replace Defeat the Leader and Reach the Exit. Nothing here is built yet. Tell me what to keep, cut or change, and once we've settled it, it goes to Codex as its own workstream.

What changed in this revision: standard fights are always Defeat All. The leader and exit ideas became authored special encounters you choose on the map, with two new ones beside them. The sections from Showing the rules onward haven't changed.

What's wrong today

Every fight comes down to the same loop: read the telegraph, take a free step out of it, attack, repeat.

  • The free step solves almost every warning. You get 2 free tiles of movement each turn. Boss attacks are announced one at a time, and most stay where they were aimed. There is nearly always a safe tile one or two steps away that still lets you attack. Your turns also come around faster than the dragons' (about 15–19 Time against their 17.5–20). The last dragon revision's own review said "ordinary attacks plus free sidesteps remained sufficient".
  • Bosses fight alone. Four of the six dragons have no helpers, and none of them change as the fight goes on: turn six is the same fight as turn one.
  • Guardian helpers are noise. They have 4–6 HP and die to any hit, then come back for free on every Guardian turn. Storm Cantor and Gallows Roc act at initiative 20, against a designed 11 and 9, so they rarely act at all.
  • The rules aren't shown. The Grimoire has no Guardian entries, and the pre-battle shows only tags like "Raise Terrain +2". Bespoke rules live in tooltips you have to find.
  • The leader is simply the biggest HP bar. At tier 1 that's a Tunnel Crawler 65% of the time. It's placed like everyone else and walks straight at you.
  • Exits are a coin flip. Every exit is exactly 6 tiles from your entry, and enemies are scattered around them with nothing checking the result. Nothing stops an enemy or a trap from starting on the threshold.

Five rules every encounter has to pass

  1. Two problems in one turn. Each enemy round poses at least two threats whose answers conflict. The free step can answer one of them, never both without giving up your attack.
  2. The board tightens. Pressure builds as the fight goes on, so dodging and waiting lose value.
  3. Every mechanic has a lever. Something you can see and exploit for an outsized payoff: a stagger, a self-hit, a broken shield. Hard questions need good answers.
  4. Show, then tell. A bespoke rule is visible on the board and in one plain sentence before it can hurt you. Tooltips add depth; they are never the only place a rule lives.
  5. Random, within guardrails. Setups stay random, but they come from authored roles and layouts, and a 2,000-seed sweep checks them against measured bands before they ship.

Revised 20 October, after your notes

Special encounters: fights you choose on the map

Standard fights are always Defeat All, rolled from the section's enemies the way they are today. The other objectives become four authored special encounters. Each has a fixed cast, its own rules and its own map emblem, so you know what you're walking into and can plan a route around the fights you want. Like Guardians they're harder than a standard fight and pay better, but they aren't tied to one route, and every section has its own version of each.

Each type pays in a different currency, so the map choice is about what your run needs: gear, embers or health. A clean win in any of them also earns one extra card choice and more embers than a standard fight (see Rewards).

Special encounter · replaces Defeat the Leader

The Warband: a captain behind its screen

Every section has one named captain, always the same enemy, with an escort built around it. You learn each captain's habits and plan for them.

YouEnemy CaptainScreenCommand banner Pillar
  1. A fixed captain per section. It's a titled, larger version of one of the section's own enemies (1.15× scale, a gold banner beneath it, its title on the nameplate). It has ×1.25 HP and Stoneskin 2/3/4 by tier.
  2. Back line. It starts 4–6 tiles from you, is never the closest enemy, and always has a follower standing in its way.
  3. Command. Followers within 2 tiles of the captain deal +1 damage. It's shown as a gold banner on the floor, and their intent numbers include the +1.
  4. It keeps its distance. When you get within 2, it steps back behind its screen (at most 1 tile a turn) and still acts. The Dynamo can't move, so it starts deepest in the room instead.
  5. Its order. Each captain has one signature order (below), shown in its intents like any other action.
  6. The warband breaks. When the captain dies, the followers run for the edges and vanish. One line, one sound: "The warband breaks!"
  7. Escort size 3, 4 or 5 followers by tier.

Special encounter · replaces Reach the Exit

Umbral Snare: get out before the doors seal

The shadow has trapped you in the room. Every door out is one of your routes onward, and the Umbra is closing them one at a time. A jailer holds the last one.

YouSealbinder The Umbral GaolerHunter Open doorSealing doorPillar
  1. Doors seal on the clock. Every door but one has a Sealbinder (a Veilbound Acolyte, 4 HP) channelling a black seal into it. Each sealing door shows its time on the turn clock: the first seals after about 2 of your turns, the next after about 4.
  2. Kill the Sealbinder and the door stays open. That's the lever, and it costs time you could spend running.
  3. A sealed door costs you its route. The map shows that branch sealed, and you can't take it from this room.
  4. The Umbral Gaoler holds the last door, which never seals. It's a mini-boss version of the Chainbound Gaoler: double HP, Stoneskin 2/3/4 by tier. Its Chain Reel drags you away from the threshold. You can always fight your way out.
  5. Hunters from the section come in through the door you entered, one at the start of each enemy round, up to three. Waiting only makes it worse.
  6. Escape by stepping onto an open door's threshold, as today. No door can be reached on your first turn.
  7. Where it goes: only on rooms with at least two ways onward, so a sealed door always costs a real choice.

Special encounter · new

Ember Hoarder: an escape in reverse

This time something else is running. The Ember Hoarder is a hunched, long-armed Umbra scavenger dragging a sack of stolen embers. Its gang gets in your way while it makes for a door.

YouEmber Hoarder Its gangSpilled embersIts run DoorPillar
  1. It runs. The Hoarder starts 2–3 tiles from you and flees for a door. Its route is shown, like an intent. It moves 3 tiles a turn and never attacks. Starting HP 16, 20 or 24 by tier.
  2. Pin it down. Root, Immobilize, Chill, Pull and blocking its path all slow it down. This is the fight where control cards shine.
  3. Every hit spills embers (3 per hit) onto its tile. Pick them up before the room ends, or lose them.
  4. The room ends when the Hoarder dies or gets out. Its gang flees either way, and nobody has to kill them.
  5. If it escapes, you keep what you picked up and get a standard reward, with no special bonus.
  6. New art: the Hoarder is the one new creature in this proposal. It goes through the visual identity gate like any board art, and a stand-in is used until it's approved.

Special encounter · brings back Survive

Last Light: keep the brazier burning

The parked Survive objective, now with a reason. The Umbra is swallowing the room and one brazier still burns. Keep it lit until the marked time and the dark recedes.

YouBrazier (flames) Its LightEnemyHidden in the darkHits next turn Pillar
  1. Hold until the marked time on the turn clock, about 5 of your turns, with at least one flame lit.
  2. Snuffers. An enemy that reaches the brazier puts out one flame on its next turn instead of attacking. The snuff shows in its intent, so you get a turn to kill it, push it away or block it.
  3. The Light shrinks. The brazier lights 2 tiles around it with three flames, 1 tile with two, and only its own tile with one. Enemies outside the Light are hidden.
  4. Fire relights it. Fire damage or a Fire surface on the brazier restores a flame, up to three.
  5. Waves of section enemies come in from the edges each round. Killing them isn't the goal; holding is.
  6. Win: at the marked time the dark recedes and every enemy left dissolves.
  7. If the last flame dies, the Umbra floods the room. It becomes Defeat All in the dark, with enemies hidden beyond 1 tile of you, and you get a standard reward with no special bonus.

Rewards: better than a standard fight, below a Guardian

  • Every clean win offers 4 cards instead of 3, at least one of them Rare or better, and 1.5× the embers of a standard fight at that depth.
  • Plus a bonus in the type's own currency:
    • Warband, Captain's spoils: a guaranteed equipment drop.
    • Umbral Snare, Slipped the snare: +6 embers for each door still open when you leave, yours included.
    • Ember Hoarder, the hoard: killing it drops another standard fight's worth of embers, on top of everything it spilled.
    • Last Light, Kindled: heal 2 HP for each flame still lit, up to 6.
  • Fail the goal (the Hoarder escapes, or the last flame dies) and you still finish the room, with a standard reward.
  • Guardians keep their unique trophies. Special encounters never award relics.

Where they appear

  • Every section has 2 or 3, and never two of the same type. Over a run you meet each type three to five times, each time as that section's version.
  • They replace standard fights and count as fights in every route's fight budget, so route lengths and difficulty totals don't change.
  • Not in the opening two steps, and never directly before or after a Guardian on the same route.
  • Every route passes at most two, and at least two different routes have one, so there's always a path with them and a path without.
  • Umbral Snare only goes where the room has at least two ways onward.
  • Unknown rooms never hide a special encounter. You always know when you're walking into one.
  • In the Heart of the Umbra, special encounters are landmarks that show through the fog, like Guardians.
  • Difficulty follows the room's depth, like standard fights.

Shared by dragons and Guardians

Showing the rules before they hurt you

  • Next three intents. Boss rotations are fixed, so the bar under the boss's health shows the next three in order. You can plan ahead: Faultline is next, so break the spires now.
  • First-sight callout. The first time a bespoke rule appears in a profile, the camera settles on the tiles it affects and a short card names it, says what it does and how to beat it. Any key dismisses it, and it goes into the Grimoire. It never appears again unless you turn it back on.
  • Guardians become proper mini-bosses. They get a compact boss bar with a signature badge and the next-three strip, and the Umbra never hides them (their helpers can still hide).
  • Grimoire entries for all six Guardians and their helpers; there are none today.

The six dragons

Three acts, and a cataclysm you can break

  1. Lieutenants. Each dragon brings two helpers that feed its mechanic. They're worth killing, but every card you spend on them isn't spent on the dragon. They don't come back in act one; one fresh lieutenant joins at Wrath.
  2. Wrath at half health. One beat: a roar, a screen shake and the name banner. The arena changes for good and the rotation gets harder for act two.
  3. The cataclysm. At Wrath the dragon channels its cataclysm. It hits almost the whole board next turn, except the shelter its own mechanic creates.
    • Break meter: 14 damage, plus 2 per section, to start; tunable. Fill it before the cataclysm lands and the dragon is broken: it loses its next action and your next hit on it deals +6.
    • Miss it and the cataclysm lands.
    • If act two drags on, the cataclysm comes back as the fifth step of its rotation.
  4. The same language for all six. Shelter, Break and Wrath work the same way on every dragon. You learn the system once; each dragon answers it differently.

The six Guardians

One sharp rule each, and helpers that serve it

  • Signature rule. Every Guardian has one always-on rule, shown as a badge on its bar and in the pre-battle.
  • Finite helpers. Two at the start. Replacements come only from a telegraphed Call intent, once per rotation, instead of a free summon on every Guardian turn.
  • Fury at half health. One sharp escalation and one banner.
  • Pace. Storm Cantor and Gallows Roc go back to their designed initiative (20 now, 11 and 9 designed), then get tuned from the bot runs.

Your calls

Each area has its own item in the Review Inbox, so you can approve or redirect them separately.

  1. Special encounters. Are these four the right set? Any type, captain or section twist you'd cut or change?
  2. Rewards. An extra card choice, plus gear, embers or healing by type: about the right level?
  3. How often. Two or three per section, never the same type twice in one section?
  4. The Ember Hoarder needs one new creature. Worth a full art pass, or should it be a laden version of an existing enemy?
  5. Showing the rules. Is the next-three strip welcome or too much? Is a one-time callout that waits for a key OK? Do you want How it fights on boss pre-battles, given you trimmed that screen before?
  6. Dragons. Three acts with a breakable cataclysm for all six? Any dragon idea you'd change? Tharokh first?
  7. Guardians. One sharp rule each, with finite helpers? Any of the six rules you'd swap?
