What's wrong today

Every fight comes down to the same loop: read the telegraph, take a free step out of it, attack, repeat.

  • The free step solves almost every warning. You get 2 free tiles of movement each turn. Boss attacks are announced one at a time, and most stay where they were aimed. There is nearly always a safe tile one or two steps away that still lets you attack. Your turns also come around faster than the dragons' (about 15–19 Time against their 17.5–20). The last dragon revision's own review said "ordinary attacks plus free sidesteps remained sufficient".
  • Bosses fight alone. Four of the six dragons have no helpers, and none of them change as the fight goes on: turn six is the same fight as turn one.
  • Guardian helpers are noise. They have 4–6 HP and die to any hit, then come back for free on every Guardian turn. Storm Cantor and Gallows Roc act at initiative 20, against a designed 11 and 9, so they rarely act at all.
  • The rules aren't shown. The Grimoire has no Guardian entries, and the pre-battle shows only tags like "Raise Terrain +2". Bespoke rules live in tooltips you have to find.
  • The leader is simply the biggest HP bar. At tier 1 that's a Tunnel Crawler 65% of the time. It's placed like everyone else and walks straight at you.
  • Exits are a coin flip. Every exit is exactly 6 tiles from your entry, and enemies are scattered around them with nothing checking the result. Nothing stops an enemy or a trap from starting on the threshold.

Five rules every encounter has to pass

  1. Two problems in one turn. Each enemy round poses at least two threats whose answers conflict. The free step can answer one of them, never both without giving up your attack.
  2. The board tightens. Pressure builds as the fight goes on, so dodging and waiting lose value.
  3. Every mechanic has a lever. Something you can see and exploit for an outsized payoff: a stagger, a self-hit, a broken shield. Hard questions need good answers.
  4. Show, then tell. A bespoke rule is visible on the board and in one plain sentence before it can hurt you. Tooltips add depth; they are never the only place a rule lives.
  5. Random, within guardrails. Setups stay random, but they come from authored roles and layouts, and a 2,000-seed sweep checks them against measured bands before they ship.

Defeat the Leader

The Warband: the leader is the brain, behind a screen

The question each fight should ask: punch through the screen now, or survive a stronger warband while you work around it?

  1. Leader by role, not HP. Casters and support lead first (Dust Acolyte, Grave Surgeon, Hoarfrost Seer, Gale Herald, Briar Weaver, Slag Mortar, Hollow Mimic), then ranged (Bone Harrier, Frostglass Lancer), then heavies (Stone Warden, Chainbound Gaoler, Glacial Mirror). Fodder never leads (Crawler, Ooze, Droplet, Tick, Wisp), and neither do the immobile Capacitor or the hiders (Gloam Stalker, Burrow Grub). The pick is weighted random among the two best available roles, so it varies.
  2. Back line. It starts 4–6 tiles from you, is never the closest enemy, and always has a follower standing in its way.
  3. Command. Followers within 2 tiles of the leader deal +1 damage. It's shown as a gold banner on the floor, and their intent numbers include the +1.
  4. It keeps its distance. When you get within 2, it steps back behind its screen (at most 1 tile a turn) and still acts.
  5. Fairer buff. ×1.25 HP and Stoneskin 2/3/4 by tier, down from ×1.5 and 3/4/5. The challenge comes from position, not from a sponge.
  6. The warband breaks. When the leader dies, the followers run for the edges and vanish, matching the "the rest flee" text. Today they silently collapse, and Ember Ticks still burst. One line, one sound: "The warband breaks!"
  7. War chest. The leader drops embers worth half of what the fleeing followers were worth. Leader rooms currently pay half of a Defeat All room.

Guardrails (2,000 seeds per tier): the leader isn't the closest enemy in at least 95% of rooms and has a screen in at least 85%; no enemy type is more than 35% of a tier's leaders; reaching it takes a median deck 2–3 turns.

Reach the Exit

The Breakout: hunters behind you, a gatekeeper at the door

An escape should be a chase with a real route choice: the short lane past the gatekeeper, or the long way round while the hunters close in. Every room is escapable in 2–4 turns, and none is a free walk-out.

  1. Roles. Gatekeepers (Stone Warden, Chainbound Gaoler, Glacial Mirror, Briar Weaver), at most one per door, start 2 tiles inside it and hold its lane. Hunters (Tunnel Crawler, Ash Hound, Gust Shrike, Bone Harrier) start 3 tiles behind or beside you and chase.
  2. Lanes. Escape rooms get their own pillar and crate layouts, with two routes to each door: short past the gatekeeper, or long around cover.
  3. Guardrails, checked on every room. If a check fails, the layout is rerolled up to 20 times, then a safe authored layout is used:
    • nothing starts on a threshold: no enemy, trap or loot;
    • every door is contested, so there's never a turn-1 route out that no enemy can reach;
    • at least one door has a route that a median deck clears in 2–4 turns, facing at most two attacks on the way;
    • no door has two gatekeepers.
  4. Gatekeepers block. Gatekeepers make blocking the route to their door their main goal. Today that's only the last tie-breaker in enemy movement.
  5. Clean escape pays. Escaping earns half the embers of the enemies still standing. Loot sits on the contested lane, so the risk pays too. Exit rooms currently pay the least of all (median 10 embers).
  6. Option, your call: hold the door. Reaching the threshold opens the door, and you escape at the start of your next turn if you're still standing on it. That ends 1-turn escapes and makes the Gaoler's pull a real threat, but it adds one enemy round to every escape. I lean yes.

Shared by dragons and Guardians

Showing the rules before they hurt you

  • Next three intents. Boss rotations are fixed, so the bar under the boss's health shows the next three in order. You can plan ahead: Faultline is next, so break the spires now.
  • First-sight callout. The first time a bespoke rule appears in a profile, the camera settles on the tiles it affects and a short card names it, says what it does and how to beat it. Any key dismisses it, and it goes into the Grimoire. It never appears again unless you turn it back on.
  • Guardians become proper mini-bosses. They get a compact boss bar with a signature badge and the next-three strip, and the Umbra never hides them (their helpers can still hide).
  • Grimoire entries for all six Guardians and their helpers; there are none today.

The six dragons

Three acts, and a cataclysm you can break

  1. Lieutenants. Each dragon brings two helpers that feed its mechanic. They're worth killing, but every card you spend on them isn't spent on the dragon. They don't come back in act one; one fresh lieutenant joins at Wrath.
  2. Wrath at half health. One beat: a roar, a screen shake and the name banner. The arena changes for good and the rotation gets harder for act two.
  3. The cataclysm. At Wrath the dragon channels its cataclysm. It hits almost the whole board next turn, except the shelter its own mechanic creates.
    • Break meter: 14 damage, plus 2 per section, to start; tunable. Fill it before the cataclysm lands and the dragon is broken: it loses its next action and your next hit on it deals +6.
    • Miss it and the cataclysm lands.
    • If act two drags on, the cataclysm comes back as the fifth step of its rotation.
  4. The same language for all six. Shelter, Break and Wrath work the same way on every dragon. You learn the system once; each dragon answers it differently.

The six Guardians

One sharp rule each, and helpers that serve it

  • Signature rule. Every Guardian has one always-on rule, shown as a badge on its bar and in the pre-battle.
  • Finite helpers. Two at the start. Replacements come only from a telegraphed Call intent, once per rotation, instead of a free summon on every Guardian turn.
  • Fury at half health. One sharp escalation and one banner.
  • Pace. Storm Cantor and Gallows Roc go back to their designed initiative (20 now, 11 and 9 designed), then get tuned from the bot runs.
