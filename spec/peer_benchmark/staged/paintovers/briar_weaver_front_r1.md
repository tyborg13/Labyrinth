Use your built-in image generation tool (real image generation, not code drawing). Make ONE image: a square 1024x1024 PNG.

## References are for camera, scale and hand only
Never copy a reference's pose, silhouette, species or equipment. The new subject must have its own distinct silhouette
that could not be mistaken for any reference at game size.

## Scale and detail (most important)
The reference images are sprites from this game enlarged 4x with hard pixel edges: every small square block you see in
them is ONE game pixel (4x4 image pixels). Paint the new subject in exactly the same block size and the same hand:
- every colour patch is at least one 4x4 block; contours step in 4-pixel blocks; outline is one dark block wide.
- the same LOW level of detail as the references: a few big, chunky, readable masses per body part. Nothing thinner
  than one block. No fine texture, no thin strands or filaments, no tiny repeated details (scales, stitches, chain
  links, rivets, cracks) unless each piece is at least 3 blocks wide.
- The image will be shrunk 4x back to game size; whatever needs a block smaller than 4 pixels will vanish, so leave it out.

## Proportions (most important change)
Follow the maquette's proportions exactly: a hunched bramble creature with a deep hood, a shaggy mantle of torn
leaves over big shoulders, long gnarled root arms ending in big hooked-claw hands, and short thick twisted root legs on
clawed root feet. The hunch is in the pose, not a short body.

## Palette and light
Dark, earthy browns: bark #2b1d15 to #5a3a24, leaves #3a2a1a to #6b4a2e with a few dry highlights #8a6440, the
thorns a pale bone-brown #a58a66. Light from the upper left; shadows warm and deep. The ONE accent colour is the single
glowing green eye (#7fd35a) deep inside the hood. No olive or green on the leaves.

## Background
Perfectly flat opaque chroma green #00FF00 (RGB 0,255,0) on every background pixel, including gaps between limbs.
No floor, ground, cast shadow, glow halo, vignette, scenery, border, text or labels. Nothing touches the canvas edge.

## Structure (most important): paint over the maquette
The first reference is a simple 3D maquette of this exact pose, rendered from the game camera. Its flat colours are PART
LABELS, not paint colours. Keep every part where the maquette puts it: the silhouette, size, overlaps, camera angle and
which side each limb is on. Turn the smooth maquette shapes into the painted creature, but never move, merge or invent
parts, and keep each part visibly separate where the maquette separates it (the rig will cut along these parts).
Labels: yellow = the hood and the head inside it; coral red = the torso with the leaf mantle and its thorns;
dark red = the pelvis; pale pink = the leaf tatters hanging below the mantle; blues (mid to very pale) = the near arm (toward the viewer, on the image's right): upper arm, forearm, hand; purple to pale lilac = the far arm (on the image's left): upper arm, forearm, hand; teal tones = the legs (thigh, shin and foot; the lighter teals are the near leg, toward the viewer).
The second reference shows the same maquette's 3D form in grey. Do not reproduce the label colours.

## Forms: follow the maquette
Keep the hood's draped, torn edge and the deep shadow of its face opening (one green eye inside), the layered
clumps of torn leaves on the mantle, the thorns on the shoulders and back, the twisted root texture of the limbs and the
big hooked claws. Leaf clumps are big jagged masses, never thin veins or fringes.

## Identity and materials
The third reference is this creature's current painting: keep its identity and materials (bark, dry leaves,
thorns, the hood, the one green eye), NOT its pose, size or detail level. The fourth reference (the approved Frostglass
Lancer) shows how this game paints figures now; the fifth (the hero) shows the hand, block size and level of detail to
match (both enlarged 4x).
