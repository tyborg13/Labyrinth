# Driving Tactical Loop - Version 3 Notes

## Purpose and version preservation

This is a separate audition draft made in response to two observations about version 2:

1. the stronger cello gave the arrangement the desired darker center, but some violin activity remained too quiet;
2. a restrained driving beat might help the cello move tactical action forward.

Nothing from versions 1 or 2 is overwritten. The new MIDI and previews use the `driving_tactical_loop*` names, and the version-3 verifier locks the SHA-256 hashes of the earlier MIDI, Ogg, FLAC, reports, notes, and version-2 string-bank files.

## What remains unchanged from version 2

- The source is the same unchanged OpenScore CC0 `.mxl` documented in `LICENSE_SOURCE.md`.
- The selected material, whole-measure boundaries, 92 QPM tempo, and A-B-C-B-A form are unchanged.
- All 3,212 string events remain: 953 Violin I, 792 Violin II, 665 viola, 406 cello, and 396 octave-below cello/bass notes.
- Every string pitch, start, gate duration, source coordinate, source part, and section assignment is identical to version 2.
- The viola, grave cello, and undercrypt-bass specifications are byte-for-byte-equivalent dataclass settings to version 2.
- The cello keeps its +10 MIDI-velocity treatment, 108% gate target subject to monophony, 0.82 render gain, dark sample, panning, release, and restrained vibrato.
- The bass remains an exact octave-below derivation of eligible cello notes; no independent bass line is added.
- The version-2 procedural string WAVs are reused unchanged and verified by hash.
- The dark three-tap string echo and one-measure loop crossfade are unchanged.

## Violin balance adjustment

No violin note is added, removed, reharmonized, lengthened, or moved. Only MIDI velocity, MIDI fallback volume, and render gain change:

| Part | Version-2 render gain | Version-3 render gain | Gain change | MIDI velocity change | MIDI fallback volume |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Violin I | 0.42 | 0.52 | +1.855 dB | +3 | 78 to 86 |
| Violin II | 0.32 | 0.43 | +2.566 dB | +4 | 68 to 78 |

Because the renderer's velocity curve is nonlinear, the combined per-note sample amplitude increases approximately 31.6-37.5% for Violin I and 46.5-57.2% for Violin II, depending on the original note velocity. The second violin receives the larger correction because it was the more deeply recessed of the two.

The resulting pre-master stem RMS remains intentionally bottom-heavy:

- cello plus bass: approximately 0.122;
- Violin I plus Violin II: approximately 0.045;
- percussion: approximately 0.016.

Those measurements demonstrate the mix policy, not a claim about human perception: the low strings still dominate average energy, while the violins and drum attacks remain individually audible enough to judge by ear.

## Percussion design

The percussion is new arrangement material, kept on MIDI channel 10 (zero-based channel 9) and on one separately named track. It does not alter Schubert's harmonic or melodic pitch content.

Three one-shot instruments are generated locally:

- **Low war drum / MIDI 36:** downbeats and beat 3, with a slightly stronger first bar of each form section;
- **Muted bone tom / MIDI 41:** a restrained beat-4 response in A; beats 2 and 4 in B and C;
- **Ash tick / MIDI 42:** quiet offbeat motion, two hits per A-section measure and four per B/C measure.

This creates a steady half-time foundation under the cello. A is intentionally sparser; B and C gain a fuller offbeat pulse to match their more active string writing. There are 736 MIDI percussion events across the 4:31 structural sequence, but their short gates, very low tick gain, and sparse voicing keep maximum simultaneous playback to five melodic voices plus one percussion voice.

The drum one-shots are kept substantially drier than the strings so their timing remains legible. They receive only a three-sample reconstruction filter after 16 kHz playback; the dark circular echo remains on the strings rather than washing out the pulse.

## Procedural percussion and rights

`procedural_percussion_bank/` contains three deterministic mono 16 kHz PCM WAVs generated by `scripts/build_driving_tactical_loop.py`. They use swept sine oscillators, mathematical envelopes, fixed integer-noise seeds, and deliberate 8-10-bit signal quantization. No recording, performer sample, SoundFont, sample pack, ROM or game rip, commercial MIDI, modern arrangement, or model-generated audio is used.

See `DRIVING_PERCUSSION_PROVENANCE.md` and `procedural_percussion_bank/bank_manifest.json` for the full evidence, generator parameters, and hashes.

## Audio and loop behavior

- Structural MIDI duration: 416 quarters / approximately 4:31.304.
- Seamless Ogg/FLAC duration: 412 quarters / approximately 4:28.696.
- Format: 44.1 kHz stereo Ogg Vorbis plus lossless FLAC.
- Peak: approximately -7.5 dBFS.
- Independent Mediabunny 1.55.2 duration: 268.701 seconds for Ogg and 268.696 seconds for FLAC; both report 44.1 kHz stereo with the expected Vorbis/FLAC codec.
- The audio begins one measure after the structural MIDI's nominal opening because the existing tail-to-head crossfade is rotated to the end of the rendered file.
- The string echo remains circular; all percussion one-shots fade to zero before their file ends.

A full-file waveform and log-frequency spectrogram inspection found no clipping plateau, unplanned long gap, or broadband drum layer masking the pitched upper-string bands. Automated analysis independently reports no silence longer than 750 ms at -60 dBFS.

`DRIVING_TACTICAL_LOOP_VERIFICATION.json` independently checks the original source hash, prior-version hashes, exact string retention and source mapping, channel isolation, drum pattern, generated WAV formats and hashes, strict container decoding, peak, silence, duration, and decoded loop seam.

## Rebuild

From this POC directory, after installing `requirements.txt` in Python 3.12+:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_driving_tactical_loop.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_driving_tactical_loop.py
```

Pass `--skip-audio` to generate only the version-3 MIDI and percussion bank. The build reads but never writes the prior string bank or any version-1/version-2 audition artifact.

## Audition questions

1. Are the two violin lines now continuously legible without pulling the emotional center away from the cello?
2. Is the low drum sufficiently driving, or should the pulse be heavier/lighter?
3. Are the offbeat ash ticks useful motion, or would a barer war-drum-and-tom pattern better suit long tactical sessions?
